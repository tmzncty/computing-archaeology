# Acknowledgements — Manufacturing Completion Tranche

This tranche exists to preserve work that conventional computer history often compresses into phrases such as “reliability improved,” “lead-free assembly was adopted,” or “high-speed boards required better materials.”

Those phrases hide people.

## Device-reliability work

We acknowledge the engineers, physicists, technicians and failure analysts who built the evidence base around:

- TDDB stress structures and oxide breakdown statistics;
- hot-carrier stress and transistor parameter drift;
- BTI stress/recovery timing and measurement protocol;
- accelerated-temperature and voltage qualification;
- Weibull/statistical reliability analysis;
- compact aging-model integration into design flows.

Their work made lifetime an engineering variable rather than a post-field-failure surprise.

## Memory soft-error and low-alpha materials work

We acknowledge:

- T. C. May and M. H. Woods for the landmark 1979 alpha-soft-error analysis;
- package-material chemists and ceramic/filler suppliers;
- alpha-counting and radiation-measurement technicians;
- memory reliability teams;
- ECC, parity and system-recovery designers;
- supplier-quality teams that qualified low-alpha materials.

A stored bit became reliable partly because people learned to measure trace radioactivity in materials that looked mechanically inert.

## Moisture-sensitive packaging work

We acknowledge:

- molding-compound and package-material teams;
- moisture-diffusion and delamination researchers;
- dry-pack, desiccant and humidity-indicator suppliers;
- bake-oven and dry-cabinet operators;
- factory floor-life tracking and material-control staff;
- reflow-profile engineers;
- cross-section and acoustic-microscopy failure analysts;
- IPC/JEDEC committee participants who standardized moisture/reflow handling.

The best moisture-control work is often invisible because nothing pops.

## Lead-free transition work

We acknowledge:

- NIST and consortium researchers who resisted simplistic alloy-ranking claims;
- solder-alloy metallurgists;
- powder and paste suppliers;
- flux chemists;
- reflow-profile engineers;
- PCB laminate and surface-finish suppliers;
- package-reliability laboratories;
- RoHS compliance and exemption teams;
- manufacturing engineers who converted entire factories while keeping products shippable.

The transition was not a single alloy substitution. It was a coordinated industrial migration.

## Tin-whisker risk work

We acknowledge NASA NEPP and the wider reliability community that preserved whisker photographs, anecdotes and failure histories, as well as:

- plating engineers;
- finish suppliers;
- procurement specialists;
- conformal-coating engineers;
- long-duration reliability testers;
- microscopy/failure-analysis teams;
- people who tracked failures that appeared years or decades after production.

## PCB electrochemical reliability

We acknowledge the engineers and technicians behind:

- CAF coupons and IPC-TM-650 methods;
- laminate/resin/glass qualification;
- drilling-process studies;
- SIR testing;
- ion chromatography;
- ROSE/extract testing;
- temperature-humidity-bias chambers;
- flux-residue characterization;
- dendrite and CAF failure cross-sections;
- board cleaning and rinse-process control.

They showed that “insulator” and “clean” are measured process states, not visual descriptions.

## Connector and socket work

We acknowledge:

- Morton Antler and the wider electrical-contact / tribology community;
- spring-contact metallurgists;
- plating and underplating engineers;
- connector-lubricant researchers;
- contact-force and wipe-geometry designers;
- mixed-flow-gas test technicians;
- socket molding and stamping teams;
- LGA socket designers and validation teams;
- motherboard flatness / package-warpage engineers;
- socket rework and inspection technicians.

A separable electrical interface is maintained by controlled force, wear and surface chemistry.

## Thermal-interface reliability

We acknowledge:

- TIM formulation chemists;
- filler-particle and polymer suppliers;
- thermal cycling / pump-out / dry-out test teams;
- bond-line and void-control process engineers;
- lid-attach and heatsink-interface teams;

A few micrometers of changing material can determine whether a processor throttles or survives.
