# Materials and Consumables Field Set — Source Map

This field set reconstructs the less-visible material industries that sit between a semiconductor recipe and a manufacturable computer: wet chemicals, photoresist, CMP consumables, high-purity furnace parts, fluoropolymer fluid handling, filters, gas-flow instrumentation, package-substrate dielectric films, and PCB laminate / solder systems.

It is a research map, not a substitute for article footnotes.

## Source-handling rule for this field set

Materials history is unusually vulnerable to vendor-source bias because many surviving technical details are preserved by the firms that still sell descendants of the original materials.

Therefore each source should be read according to type:

1. **period patents / papers / government industrial reports** — strongest for what a process developer or regulator documented at the time;
2. **industry-association history / standards records** — strong for standardization, committee structure, and durable terminology;
3. **museum / institutional synthesis** — useful for cross-company chronology;
4. **corporate product history** — useful for a company's own lineage, formulation format, and commercialization story, but not neutral priority or market-share evidence;
5. **modern product data** — useful for showing what properties later became formalized, not for back-projecting current specifications into the past.

## Wet chemistry and cleaning

### U.S. EPA — 1983 semiconductor industry development document

- *Development Document for Effluent Limitations Guidelines and Standards for the Electrical and Electronic Components Point Source Category, Phase I* (1983):
  https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2016-05/documents/eec_phase_1_dd_apr_1983.pdf

Use for period evidence that semiconductor manufacturing consumed HF and other acids, bases, solvents, photoresist developers/strippers, and large rinse streams.

Source type: period regulatory / industrial-process documentation.

Caveat: pollutant-control framing is not equivalent to an electronic-grade chemical specification.

### NIOSH — Siemens Components health-hazard evaluation, 1983

- https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/172024

Use for period factory evidence of acids, oxidizers, bases, and solvents in actual semiconductor polishing, etch, clean, and diffusion-related work.

Source type: period occupational-health field study.

### RCA clean lineage

The historical field should continue to recover W. Kern and D. A. Puotinen's original RCA Review publication on wafer cleaning and later Kern retrospectives.

Current navigation targets include:

- later patent discussions that cite the 1970 RCA Review paper;
- scholarly histories of silicon wafer cleaning;
- archived RCA process literature.

Use later summaries only to navigate toward original process evidence.

## Photoresist and lithographic chemistry

### Eastman Kodak — photosensitized polymer patent

- U.S. Patent 2,610,120, filed 9 March 1950, issued 9 September 1952:
  https://patents.google.com/patent/US2610120A/en

Use for primary evidence of early polyvinyl-cinnamate photosensitive resist chemistry developed for photomechanical reproduction / resist imaging.

## CMP — slurry, pad, and planarization

### IBM production paper

- H. Landis et al., “Integration of chemical-mechanical polishing into CMOS integrated circuit manufacturing,” *Thin Solid Films* 220 (1992), 1–7:
  https://doi.org/10.1016/0040-6090(92)90539-N

Use for near-primary evidence that IBM had exploited CMP in CMOS development and manufacturing since the mid-1980s and for examples of multi-level oxide / tungsten integration.

Source type: near-primary technical paper by production engineers.

### Semiconductor History Museum of Japan

- “Establishment of supply chain for CMP system and materials”:
  https://www.shmj.or.jp/english/pdf/em/exhibi2463E.pdf

Use for later synthesis of the 1990s development of CMP tool / slurry / pad supply chains and CMP's importance to planarization, STI, and copper damascene.

Source type: later museum / industry-history synthesis.

Future targets:

- IBM CMP patents and internal technical publications;
- early pad / slurry supplier literature;
- SEMATECH CMP documents;
- Cabot / Rodel / IC1000 lineage;
- post-CMP cleaning history;
- slurry filtration and pad-conditioning equipment.

## Furnace furniture — quartz and silicon carbide

### Norton — silicon-carbide diffusion-furnace components

- U.S. Patent 3,951,587, filed 6 December 1974, issued 20 April 1976:
  https://patents.justia.com/patent/3951587

Use for period technical requirements imposed on diffusion-furnace process tubes, paddles, boats, and liners: thermal shock, mechanical strength, low outgassing, low particle generation, and ultra-high chemical purity.

Source type: primary patent / vendor technical disclosure.

### High-purity SiC furnace-parts patent

- U.S. Patent 4,753,763:
  https://patents.justia.com/patent/4753763

Use for 1980s evidence that impurity removal in SiC furnace parts was itself a manufacturing problem because trapped impurities could be released at high process temperatures.

Source type: primary patent.

### Heraeus quartzware

- https://www.heraeus-covantics.com/products-and-solutions/products-by-shape/tubes/fused-quartz-and-silica-tubes-for-semiconductor-applications

Use only for mature examples of quartz reaction tubes, gas / liquid inlets, high-purity grades, and temperature-stability requirements.

Source type: modern corporate product documentation.

Do not back-project present purity grades into 1970s furnace lines.

## PCB laminate, copper foil, and solder

- https://www.ipc.org/ipc-history

Use for evidence that the printed-wiring industry organized around raw materials, solderability, through connections, multilayer boards, round-robin testing, copper-foil specifications, and acceptance standards from the late 1950s onward.

- C. A. Handwerker, “Lead-Free Solders: A Change in the Electronics Infrastructure,” 1999:
  https://www.nist.gov/publications/lead-free-solders-change-electronics-infrastructure

Use for the observation that electronics assembly infrastructure had become deeply organized around tin-lead solder and that changing the alloy affected the wider manufacturing system.

Source type: technical / institutional analysis.

## Preservation priority

Materials evidence disappears quickly because production lines consume it.

Particularly valuable artifacts include:

```text
chemical certificates of analysis
photoresist bottles / technical sheets
slurry drums and pad data sheets
used furnace tubes / boats / liners
PFA valves, fittings, and tubing samples
filter housings and used filter cartridges
MFC manuals and calibration sheets
package-substrate coupons
copper-clad laminate samples
prepreg sheets
copper foil specifications
solder / flux technical sheets
incoming-inspection records
supplier-change qualification reports
```

A finished processor contains almost none of this evidence, yet its manufacturability depended on all of it.
